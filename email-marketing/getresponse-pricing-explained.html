GetResponse's pricing page lists four tiers — Starter, Marketer, Creator, and Enterprise — priced against your contact count, with the numbers rising as your list grows. This breaks down what's actually included in each, based directly on GetResponse's own pricing page, and flags the gaps that aren't obvious from the marketing copy.

GetResponse's pricing page displayed these figures in EUR at the time of this review; GetResponse localizes pricing by region, so a US or other non-EU visitor may see a different currency and number — always check the live page before budgeting. Yearly billing is advertised as an 18% saving versus paying monthly, per the pricing page. All tiers scale price up as your contact count grows past 1,000 — the numbers above are the entry point, not a flat rate regardless of list size.

There's no permanent free plan

This is the detail most likely to surprise someone comparing GetResponse against competitors like Mailchimp or Kit, both of which offer indefinite free tiers. GetResponse's "free" offering is a 14-day trial with premium features unlocked, per its pricing page, not a forever-free plan. During the trial, certain features are already scoped down — landing pages are capped at 1,000 unique visitors/month, webinars are capped at 10 participants, and outgoing messages carry GetResponse branding that's only removable on a paid plan. After the trial ends without upgrading, you're left with a further-restricted free account, not full trial access.

If you're comparison-shopping specifically for a no-cost starting point, treat GetResponse as "pay within two weeks or lose functionality" rather than "free forever with limits," which is a meaningfully different commitment than some competitors ask for.

Starter: the entry paid tier, with real limits

Starter is the cheapest paid plan, and per the pricing page it includes:

  • Unlimited email sends
  • Up to 3 uses of GetResponse's AI content generator
  • 1 custom automation workflow
  • Unlimited landing pages, with 3 AI landing-page-creator uses included

The one-workflow cap on automation is the detail worth pausing on. If your plan for GetResponse involves more than a single automated sequence — say, a welcome flow and a separate re-engagement flow — Starter won't cover it, and you'll need to move to Marketer or above. This is a common reason evaluators find Starter cheaper on paper than in practice: the price looks attractive until you hit the automation ceiling.

Marketer and Creator: where automation opens up

Both Marketer (EUR 54/mo) and Creator (EUR 62/mo) tiers include unlimited automation workflows and unlimited AI tool usage, per the pricing page — removing the constraints that make Starter feel thin. The distinguishing feature between these two tiers is webinars: Creator adds webinar hosting for up to 100 attendees with 3-hour recording storage, which Marketer does not include (per GetResponse's own tier comparison — webinars aren't listed under Starter or Marketer at all).

So the practical decision between Marketer and Creator comes down to one question: do you need webinars? If yes, Creator's added roughly EUR 8/mo (at the 1,000-contact tier) buys that capability. If you don't plan to run webinars, Marketer gets you the same unlimited automation and AI tooling for less.

Enterprise: the tier for features that aren't available anywhere else

Enterprise is custom-priced — GetResponse doesn't publish a number, you have to book a demo. What's notable is which features are gated exclusively behind Enterprise, per the pricing page: SMS marketing, transactional email, mobile push notifications, single sign-on, and dedicated support. None of these are available at any price on Starter, Marketer, or Creator. If your use case specifically needs transactional email or SSO for a team, you're not choosing a tier by contact volume — you're forced to Enterprise regardless of list size, which means talking to sales rather than self-serve signup.

Enterprise also comes with an expanded webinar allowance (up to 1,000 attendees, 20-hour recording storage per the pricing page) and, per GetResponse, a separate 30-day pilot program with dedicated support available through the demo-booking process.

What GetResponse doesn't clearly disclose on the pricing page

A few things worth flagging as analysis, not fact, since they weren't stated in dollar/feature terms on the pricing page itself: GetResponse's pricing page doesn't show you the tier cost at higher contact counts (5,000, 10,000, 50,000 subscribers) without either using an interactive slider or entering your own contact count — the EUR 16/54/62 figures above are 1,000-contact anchor prices only, and will rise as your list grows. If you're evaluating GetResponse for a list that's already past a few thousand contacts, don't budget off the entry-tier numbers in this article — check the live, interactive pricing calculator for your actual list size.

Annual billing versus monthly: is the 18% worth it?

GetResponse's pricing page advertises an 18% discount for paying yearly across the tiers shown. Whether that's worth committing to depends on how confident you are in the platform before you've used it — since there's no permanent free plan to test long-term on, you're weighing a 14-day trial against a 12-month commitment. For a business with an established list and a track record of using GetResponse's trial successfully, the annual discount is a straightforward savings. For someone still evaluating whether GetResponse's automation model and lack of a free tier fit their workflow, it's reasonable to pay monthly for the first few months and switch to annual billing once you're confident the tier you've chosen actually covers your needs — the discount doesn't disappear if you upgrade later, but you also don't want to prepay a year on a tier you discover is too limited.

Comparing tiers side by side on what actually changes

It's easy to assume each tier up simply adds "more" of everything, but the changes are specific, per GetResponse's pricing page:

  • Starter → Marketer: automation workflows go from 1 to unlimited, and AI tool usage goes from capped (3 uses) to unlimited. This is the single biggest functional jump in the whole pricing ladder — Starter's core limitation is removed entirely at Marketer.
  • Marketer → Creator: the only stated addition is webinar hosting (up to 100 attendees). If you don't need webinars, there's no functional reason to pay the Creator premium over Marketer.
  • Creator → Enterprise: this isn't an incremental upgrade so much as a different product tier — SMS, transactional email, push notifications, and SSO are all net-new capabilities not available at any lower tier, alongside expanded webinar capacity and dedicated support.

Understanding that the Marketer-to-Creator jump is narrow (webinars only) while the Starter-to-Marketer jump is broad (automation and AI both unlocked) should shape how you prioritize your budget: most businesses get more practical value moving from Starter to Marketer than from Marketer to Creator, unless webinars specifically are part of your plan.

Choose Starter if:

  • Your list is small, you don't need more than one automated sequence running, and you mainly want unlimited sends and basic landing pages.

Choose Marketer if:

  • You need multiple automation workflows and don't care about webinars.

Choose Creator if:

  • You want the same unlimited automation as Marketer, plus native webinar hosting, and the roughly EUR 8/mo premium (at the 1,000-contact tier) is worth it versus running a separate webinar tool.

Choose Enterprise if:

  • You specifically need SMS, transactional email, SSO, or dedicated support — these aren't available at any lower tier regardless of budget, so the decision isn't really about price at that point.

The bottom line

GetResponse's pricing is straightforward at a glance but has two real gotchas worth knowing before you sign up: there's no permanent free plan (just a 14-day trial), and the cheapest paid tier caps you at a single automation workflow, which undersells what "Starter" sounds like it should include. If automation depth matters to you, budget for Marketer or Creator from the start rather than assuming you can begin on Starter and upgrade painlessly later — the entry tier is priced for a genuinely minimal use case.
