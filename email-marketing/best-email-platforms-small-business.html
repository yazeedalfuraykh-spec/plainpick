There isn't one "best" email platform for a small business — there's a best platform for your specific list size, budget, and whether you need things like webinars, e-commerce automation, or transactional email alongside your newsletter. This guide compares five widely used platforms — GetResponse, Kit, MailerLite, Brevo, and Mailchimp — on verified facts from each vendor's pricing page, then walks through how to actually decide.

Start with your list size and send frequency, not the platform's reputation

Before comparing feature lists, work out two numbers: how many contacts you have (or expect within 3-6 months), and roughly how often you'll email them. These two numbers eliminate options faster than any feature comparison.

Under 250 contacts, infrequent sending: Mailchimp's free plan (500 sends/month or 250/day, per its pricing page) or MailerLite's free plan (2,500 emails/month for up to 250 active subscribers, per its pricing page) both cover this without cost. MailerLite's free-tier send allowance is meaningfully larger than Mailchimp's at the same contact ceiling, which matters if you send more than roughly one email a week to your full list.

Under 10,000 contacts, want to build for free while growing: Kit's free plan (per its pricing page) is the standout here — no other platform in this comparison offers a free tier anywhere close to 10,000 subscribers. The catch, per Kit's tier breakdown, is that automations aren't included on the free plan, only broadcasts and list-building tools.

Need automation from day one, small list: This is where free plans stop being sufficient regardless of platform. GetResponse's Starter tier includes automation but caps you at one workflow; MailerLite's free plan includes 3 automations (per its pricing page), which may be enough for a single welcome sequence; Kit requires the paid Creator tier for any automation at all.

Feature areas where these platforms genuinely differ

Webinars: GetResponse is the only platform in this group that bundles webinar hosting natively (Creator tier and above, capped at 100 attendees, expanding to 1,000 on Enterprise, per its pricing page). None of Kit, MailerLite, or Mailchimp list native webinar hosting on their pricing pages. If webinars are core to how you nurture leads, this narrows your choice significantly — you'd otherwise be paying for a separate webinar tool (Zoom Webinar, Livestorm, etc.) alongside whichever ESP you choose.

Creator commerce / paid newsletters: Kit is built around this — "Sell digital products & subscriptions" and paid newsletter support appear on every tier including free, per Kit's pricing page. If your business model is selling directly to subscribers, this is a meaningful structural advantage over platforms that treat commerce as a bolt-on.

Automation depth and count: MailerLite scales automation count with plan tier (3 on free, 50 on Comfort, unlimited on Power, per its pricing page), which gives you a clearer sense of what you're buying at each step than GetResponse's binary "1 workflow, then unlimited" jump between Starter and Marketer.

AI tooling: Both GetResponse (from Starter, limited to 3 uses; unlimited from Marketer) and MailerLite (AI writing assistant on Comfort tier) advertise AI content tools, per their respective pricing pages. Mailchimp lists generative AI as included at its Standard tier. Treat AI-tool claims as vendor-stated features rather than judgments about output quality — none of these vendors publish independent quality benchmarks, and we haven't tested any of them ourselves.

Pricing transparency over time: This is analysis, not a quoted fact from any single page, but worth flagging: Mailchimp's advertised Essentials/Standard/Premium prices are explicitly promotional for the first 12 months per its own pricing page, meaning your year-two cost will be higher than the number you sign up at. None of GetResponse, Kit, or MailerLite's pricing pages carried the same "promotional rate" framing in what we reviewed — their headline numbers appear to be standing prices, though you should still verify at signup since pricing structures change.

Honest downsides, one per platform

GetResponse: no permanent free plan, and the entry Starter tier's single-automation-workflow limit makes the "cheap" tier less useful than it looks for anyone planning real automation.

Kit: no automation at all on the free tier, and per-subscriber pricing rises past the 1,000-subscriber anchor used in vendor marketing — a growing list means checking the live calculator, not assuming a flat rate.

MailerLite: the free plan's 250-subscriber ceiling is tighter than Kit's 10,000-subscriber free tier, and paid-tier pricing is described as "tiered" without a single flat entry number on the pricing page — you need your actual contact count to get a real quote.

Brevo: we could not verify current pricing for this article due to a fetch failure on Brevo's JS-rendered pricing page — treat any specific number you see elsewhere about Brevo with the same skepticism you'd apply to unverified claims about any product, and check brevo.com/pricing directly.

Mailchimp: the free plan's 500-email/month cap is tight for even a modestly active small list, and the promotional pricing on paid tiers means your real ongoing cost is higher than the advertised headline number after the first year.

A decision framework, not a recommendation

Rather than naming a single "best" platform — which depends entirely on your situation — work through these questions in order:

  1. Do you need to start at $0 with no time limit? Mailchimp or MailerLite (both free plans are permanent, per their pricing pages), or Kit if your list is likely to grow past a few hundred contacts while still on zero budget (its free tier goes up to 10,000 subscribers).
  2. Do you need webinars bundled in? Only GetResponse offers this natively among the five compared here — factor in Creator tier or above.
  3. Is selling digital products or a paid newsletter your core model? Kit's commerce tooling is present on every tier, including free.
  4. Do you need automation from day one on a tight budget? MailerLite's free plan includes 3 automations, more generous than GetResponse's Starter tier (1 workflow) or Kit's free tier (none).
  5. Do you need SMS or transactional email in the same platform? GetResponse gates these to Enterprise (custom pricing); Brevo is broadly known in the industry for including SMS/transactional capability more accessibly, but we could not verify current specifics — confirm directly with Brevo.

The bottom line

For a small business with a modest list and a tight budget, MailerLite's free plan (2,500 sends/month, 3 automations included) or Kit's free plan (10,000-subscriber ceiling, no automation) are the strongest zero-cost starting points, depending on whether automation or list-size headroom matters more to you. If webinars are part of your growth plan, GetResponse is the only option here that includes them without a separate subscription. If you're already committed to paying from day one, compare actual year-two costs, not just the advertised entry price — several of these vendors use promotional first-year pricing that resets higher.
