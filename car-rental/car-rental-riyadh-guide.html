Riyadh is not a city you can walk. Distances between districts run into tens of kilometers, sidewalks are inconsistent outside a handful of neighborhoods, and summer heat rules out anything on foot for most of the year. That makes a rental car genuinely useful here, more so than in most GCC capitals. But it is also not automatic that renting is the right call for every trip, and the requirements differ depending on where your license was issued.

Who Actually Needs an International Driving Permit

Fact, per UK government travel advice (gov.uk foreign travel guidance for Saudi Arabia): a UK photocard driving licence is valid to drive in Saudi Arabia for the first three months after arrival. After that window, a local Saudi licence is required. The same guidance notes that hire car companies "may require an International Driving Permit (IDP)" on top of a foreign licence, meaning the rental company's own policy can be stricter than the general entry rule.

That pattern (foreign licence usable for a limited period, IDP sometimes demanded by the rental desk regardless) is common across countries that are not full signatories to the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic, and Saudi Arabia's exact treaty status and IDP requirement for each nationality was not confirmed through the sources available for this article.

Vendor claim: major booking platforms list "a valid driving license" as the pickup requirement for Saudi Arabia listings, without specifying IDP by nationality on the pages checked. In practice, this means the requirement varies by supplier, and the honest move is to check the specific rental company's terms for your passport country before you fly, not to assume a blanket rule.

Analysis: GCC nationals (Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, UAE) are widely reported to be able to drive in Saudi Arabia on their home-country licence without an IDP, under GCC mutual-recognition arrangements. This is common industry knowledge but could not be confirmed against an official GCC or Saudi government source in this research pass.

Requirements checklist (what to bring)

  • Valid driving licence from your home country, valid for the entire rental period
  • International Driving Permit, unless your rental company or nationality is confirmed exempt (see above; check with the specific supplier)
  • Passport and Saudi entry visa/eVisa stamp
  • A credit card in the driver's name for the deposit hold (debit cards are frequently rejected for this purpose by rental desks; confirm with your specific company)
  • Confirmation of the minimum age your chosen rental company sets. Age minimums and young-driver surcharges vary by supplier and were not consistent across the listings checked for this article, so treat any specific age or fee you see elsewhere as one company's policy, not a national rule.

Airport Pickup vs City Pickup

Analysis, not a cited fact: the practical tradeoff is straightforward even without hard data. King Khalid International Airport (RUH) sits well north of central Riyadh, and most major international and local rental brands operate counters there. Airport pickup means you drive straight into unfamiliar traffic on arrival, often at night after a long flight, which is a real fatigue and safety consideration on top of the "poor driving standards" and "high number of serious accidents" that UK travel advice explicitly flags for Saudi roads.

City-center pickup (hotel-adjacent branches, common in districts like Olaya and King Fahd Road) lets you settle in, get oriented with a taxi or ride-hailing app on day one, and pick up the car once you have a feel for the road layout. It typically costs a taxi or Careem fare to get there, against a calmer first drive.

There is no verified cost comparison available for this piece between airport and city rental rates in Riyadh; airport counters in most markets carry a location surcharge, but no dated, sourced figure for Riyadh specifically was found.

The Real Cost Factors (No Invented Numbers)

Genuinely useful cost planning for Riyadh comes down to these factors, described without invented prices:

  • Vehicle class and season. Demand spikes around major events (conferences, festivals, national holidays) push prices up industry-wide; this is standard rental market behavior, not a Riyadh-specific number we can cite.
  • Deposit size. Rental desks hold a security deposit on a credit card, refunded after a post-rental inspection. Deposit amounts vary widely by vehicle class and supplier, from what booking platforms label "low deposit" to "average deposit" tiers, without a fixed riyal figure quoted publicly.
  • Fuel. Saudi fuel prices are set nationally and are lower than most of the traveler's home markets in Europe or North America, but a specific current price was not verified for this article and should not be assumed to be current by the time you travel.
  • Cross-border driving. If you plan to cross into Bahrain, the UAE, or elsewhere, ask the rental company directly whether cross-border driving is permitted and what the authorization fee is. This is not a default inclusion.
  • Toll and traffic fines. Saudi Arabia uses automated cameras for speeding and other violations. Per gov.uk guidance, fines are issued by text message and recorded against the driver's Absher-linked identity (passport, residency, or ID), and outstanding fines must be cleared before leaving the country. Rental companies routinely pass these fines to the renter's card after the fact, sometimes with an administration fee. Confirm the fine-handling process with your rental company before you drive off.

When Renting Is Not the Better Option

This is the part most affiliate content skips, and it is worth saying plainly: for a short trip confined to central Riyadh, a rental car is not automatically the best choice.

  • If your itinerary is mostly restaurants, malls, and hotel meetings within a few districts, ride-hailing (Uber and Careem both operate widely in Riyadh) removes the parking, navigation, and unfamiliar-road-rules burden entirely, at the cost of per-trip fares instead of a flat rental rate.
  • If you are only in the city for one or two days, the time spent on paperwork, deposit holds, and fuel logistics may not pay off against a handful of taxi rides.
  • If you are not confident navigating under the driving conditions gov.uk describes as having "a high number of serious accidents," and you have the option of a driver, that is a legitimate and common choice for visitors, not a compromise.

Renting genuinely pays off when your trip involves multiple cities (Riyadh to Diriyah, AlUla, or the Eastern Province), odd-hour schedules that ride-hailing in your specific neighborhood may not serve reliably, or a stay long enough that per-trip fares would exceed a weekly rental rate. None of those comparisons can be quantified here without invented numbers, so run your own math against your actual itinerary before booking.

Booking Practically

  1. Decide airport vs city pickup based on your arrival time and how tired you expect to be, not on a price assumption you have not verified.
  2. Confirm IDP and minimum-age requirements with your specific rental company and your specific passport, in writing, before you fly.
  3. Ask directly how the company handles Absher-linked traffic fines and tolls, since these get charged after the fact.
  4. Photograph the car's existing condition at pickup, all four sides plus wheels, before you drive off. This is standard advice for any rental market and protects your deposit regardless of what the company's inspection process looks like on paper.
  5. If your trip is short and city-bound, price out ride-hailing for the same itinerary before committing to a rental. It is often the more honest answer.
