Dubai's road network is easy to drive once you know two things that trip up almost every visitor: the toll system charges you automatically with no barrier and no ticket, and the local rules around what counts as a valid license are stricter than most people assume from home.

License Requirements

Fact, per UK government travel advice (gov.uk foreign travel guidance for the UAE): a UK photocard driving licence can be used to drive in the UAE. If you hold an older paper licence, you must either upgrade it to a photocard or carry an International Driving Permit (IDP) instead. Visitors applying for UAE residency are advised to keep using their home licence until the residence permit is issued, then apply for a UAE licence from the traffic department.

This confirms the general shape of the rule for UK-issued licences specifically. The UAE is known to recognize a wide range of other countries' licenses for driving and car rental without requiring an IDP, including most GCC states, but a complete, current list by nationality was not available from the sources checked for this article.

Vendor claim, general rental industry practice: rental companies in Dubai commonly require the license to be held for a minimum period (often cited across the industry as one to two years) and set their own minimum driver age with young-driver surcharges below a threshold. Neither the exact minimum holding period nor a specific age threshold was confirmed for a named Dubai rental company in this research pass, so treat any number you see elsewhere as that company's policy, not a blanket UAE rule.

Requirements checklist

  • Valid driving licence, upgraded to photocard format if it was originally issued as paper
  • International Driving Permit if your nationality or licence type requires one; confirm directly with your rental company
  • Passport and UAE entry stamp or visa
  • Credit card in the driver's name for the deposit hold
  • Confirmation from your rental company of their minimum age and minimum license-holding period, since these are set by the company, not by a single national standard

How Salik Tolls Actually Work

Salik is Dubai's automatic toll system: there are no barriers or toll booths, and gates read your plate or windscreen tag as you pass under them at normal driving speed.

Facts, sourced from Wikipedia's article on Salik (road toll), itself citing the toll operator and press coverage:

  • The network has grown to 10 toll gates across Dubai as of the most recent expansion (a further two gates added in November 2024).
  • Salik launched in July 2007 with two initial gates, then expanded in stages through 2008, 2013, and 2018 before the 2024 additions.
  • Since 31 January 2025, Salik moved from a flat per-crossing toll to variable dynamic pricing, ranging from free during night hours up to 6 AED during peak traffic periods. The flat rate before that change was 4 AED per gate crossing.
  • The standard payment method is a prepaid RFID tag fixed to the windscreen, detected automatically by RFID and number-plate recognition cameras. A Salik tag reportedly costs 100 AED, which includes 50 AED of toll balance already loaded.
  • Since February 2013, Dubai taxis have added Salik charges directly to the metered fare rather than billing separately.

What this means for a rental car specifically: the vehicle itself, not the driver, is what the Salik cameras detect and bill. Rental companies handle this in one of a few common ways: pre-installed tag billed to your rental account, a flat daily toll-handling fee, or after-the-fact billing to your deposit card once the rental company reconciles Salik charges post-rental, sometimes with an administration fee added. Ask your specific rental company which method they use before you drive off, since this determines whether you see toll charges during the trip or as a surprise on your final bill.

Deposits and What They Actually Protect

A rental deposit is a temporary hold on your credit card, released after the company inspects the car at return and confirms no unresolved damage, fines, or toll charges. It is separate from your daily rental rate and separate from any insurance excess you agreed to.

Fact, from Rentalcars.com's stated definition: a Collision Damage Waiver (CDW) is "a type of cover that limits your liability for damage to certain parts of a rental car." The word "limits" is doing real work there. CDW is not the same as zero liability; it caps what you owe up to an excess amount, and it typically excludes specific parts of the car regardless of the waiver. See our companion piece on insurance terms for the detail on what "full coverage" usually leaves out.

Practical deposit behavior worth planning around, based on common Dubai rental market practice rather than a single cited source:

  • Deposits are usually held, not charged, meaning the amount is blocked on your card limit but not debited unless a claim is made against it.
  • Release can take one to several weeks after return, depending on your card issuer's processing time, not the rental company's.
  • Outstanding Salik tolls and Absher-style traffic fines (the UAE runs its own automated camera enforcement) are typically deducted from the deposit if unpaid at return, which is one more reason to confirm the toll-handling method up front.

Road Rules Worth Knowing Before You Drive

Facts, per UK government travel advice (gov.uk foreign travel guidance for the UAE):

  • Drink driving carries zero tolerance: "It is illegal to drink and drive, no matter how small the amount," and insurance becomes invalid if you have been drinking and have an accident.
  • After any accident, you are required to stop and call the police immediately; leaving the scene is illegal, even for a minor fender-bender.
  • Offensive gestures or profanity directed at other drivers can result in fines, jail, or deportation, a much stricter standard than most visitors expect from a traffic dispute.
  • The guidance also notes that driving standards are "not always as disciplined" as UK norms and that traffic accident rates are comparatively high, despite widespread speed cameras.
  • Police can impound a vehicle for traffic violations, and the guidance cites a release charge of 50,000 AED plus the underlying violation fines for certain impoundment cases. This figure applies to serious violations rather than routine tickets, and the specific triggering offenses were not detailed in the source.

Should You Rent at All in Dubai?

Analysis: for visitors staying mainly within central Dubai (Downtown, Marina, JBR, Business Bay), the metro and ride-hailing apps cover most tourist routes without the Salik-tracking and parking overhead of a rental car. Renting earns its cost when your itinerary spans the wider emirate, includes day trips to Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, or the desert, or runs on a schedule that public transport and ride-hailing do not serve conveniently. Weigh that against your actual itinerary rather than defaulting to a rental because it is available.
