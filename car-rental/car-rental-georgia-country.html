Georgia has become a regular short-haul destination for GCC travelers: direct or near-direct flights into Tbilisi and Kutaisi, mountain and wine-region scenery within a few hours' drive, and a visa process that is comparatively simple for many Gulf passport holders. A rental car is genuinely the better way to see most of the country outside Tbilisi itself, but the road conditions and license rules deserve more attention than most trip-planning content gives them.

License Requirements

Fact, per UK government travel advice (gov.uk foreign travel guidance for Georgia): a UK driving licence can be used to drive in Georgia for up to one year. The same guidance adds a distinction worth noting: car hire companies "may require an International Driving Permit (IDP) instead of a UK licence," meaning the rental company's own requirement can be stricter than the general legal permission to drive. The guidance also flags that IDPs cannot be purchased once you have already left the UK, so travelers are told to arrange one before departure.

That last point generalizes directly: wherever you are flying from, an International Driving Permit needs to be obtained in your home country before you travel. It cannot be arranged on arrival in Georgia.

What was not confirmed: the specific license-validity period and IDP requirement for GCC-issued licenses (Saudi, UAE, Qatari, Kuwaiti, Bahraini, and Omani licenses) were not available through the official Georgian sources checked for this article; the Ministry of Internal Affairs (police.ge) and the Georgian legislative herald (matsne.gov.ge) both returned access-denied responses on the pages attempted.

Vendor claim: rental listings for Georgia commonly show driver age brackets from 18 to 80+, with a "30-65" range appearing as a default selection on the booking platform checked, which typically signals where standard rates apply without a young or senior driver surcharge rather than a hard eligibility cutoff. Confirm your specific age bracket's surcharge, if any, with the rental company directly.

Requirements checklist

  • Valid home-country driving licence
  • International Driving Permit, arranged before you leave home, since it cannot be issued once you are in Georgia (confirm with your rental company whether it is required for your nationality)
  • Passport and any required Georgian entry documentation
  • Credit card in the driver's name for the deposit hold
  • Confirmation of your rental company's minimum age and any related surcharge

Road Conditions: The Part Most Guides Skip

This is where Georgia differs most from a GCC road trip, and it is worth planning around rather than discovering on a mountain pass at night.

Facts, per UK government travel advice:

  • Roads outside city centers are frequently "badly lit and poor quality."
  • Hazards specifically called out include stray livestock on the road, erratic driving from other motorists, and poorly maintained vehicles sharing the road with you.
  • Heavy rain, flooding, and snow at higher altitudes can affect roads and bridges, and landslides are a recognized regional hazard.
  • Road markings and right-of-way conventions are described as "confusing" even to experienced foreign drivers.
  • Georgia's drink-driving laws are stricter than UK limits, with lower blood alcohol thresholds and severe penalties; the practical advice given is to avoid alcohol entirely if you are driving.

Fact, per Wikipedia's Transport in Georgia article: only a limited number of kilometers are express roads or motorways in good condition; the quality of other roads "varies greatly." This matches the gov.uk description and is worth taking seriously if your itinerary includes mountain regions like Svaneti, Kazbegi, or Tusheti, where road quality drops sharply outside the main highway corridors.

Vendor claim, one customer review on a booking platform (not verified, presented as a single traveler's opinion, not a fact): one review stated Georgia "needs a very experienced driver because here aren't any rules." That is one person's characterization, not a sourced statistic on driving conduct, and should be read as color rather than guidance. The gov.uk facts above are the more reliable basis for planning.

Fact, per discovercars.com's Georgia rental information: the stated in-town speed limit is 50 to 70 km/h, and the out-of-town limit is 80 to 110 km/h; Georgia drives on the right-hand side of the road.

Analysis: for GCC travelers, that right-hand-side driving convention is the same as Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and the other Gulf states, so there is no side-of-the-road adjustment needed, unlike travelers coming from left-hand-drive countries. The adjustment instead is to road quality and to a mountainous, weather-affected network that most Gulf drivers have not encountered, since GCC road networks are largely flat, well-lit, and consistently paved.

Tbilisi vs Kutaisi Pickup

Most GCC-origin flights land in either Tbilisi (the capital, and the wider selection of car classes and international rental brands) or Kutaisi (a common budget-airline gateway, with a smaller but still functional rental market).

Analysis, not a cited statistic: the practical difference is route planning more than price. Kutaisi puts you closer to western Georgia (Batumi, the Black Sea coast, and parts of Svaneti) without the drive across the country from Tbilisi first. Tbilisi puts you closer to Kazbegi, Kakheti's wine region, and the eastern mountain routes. Choose pickup city based on which region you are prioritizing, not on which airport your flight happened to land at, since one-way rentals between the two cities may carry a drop-off fee that varies by rental company and was not confirmed for this article.

Cost Factors Without Invented Numbers

No specific rental prices for Georgia could be verified against a dated, sourced page for this article, so cost planning here is limited to factors rather than figures:

  • Season. Summer (Tbilisi city travel plus Black Sea coast demand) and winter (Gudauri and Bakuriani ski season) both push demand and prices up; shoulder seasons in spring and autumn are the industry-standard lower-demand windows.
  • Deposit tier. Booking platforms show a spread from "low deposit" to "no deposit" options depending on the rental company and vehicle class, without a fixed lari or dollar figure quoted consistently across suppliers.
  • Vehicle class for terrain. If your itinerary includes mountain regions, a standard sedan may not be rated for the road conditions described above; check whether your route requires 4x4 clearance before booking, since this is a route-specific decision, not a fixed rule.
  • Fuel. Georgian fuel is priced in Georgian lari and was not verified against a current, dated source for this article.

Practical Booking Steps

  1. Arrange your International Driving Permit in your home country before departure. It genuinely cannot be obtained once you land.
  2. Confirm with your specific rental company whether an IDP is required for your nationality, rather than assuming the UK-specific guidance above applies identically to your passport.
  3. If your route includes mountain regions, ask directly whether the vehicle class you are booking is appropriate for the terrain and weather you will encounter.
  4. Photograph the car's condition at pickup in both Tbilisi and Kutaisi locations; this is standard protection for your deposit regardless of which city you start from.
  5. Budget time, not just money, for road quality outside the main highways. A route that looks short on a map can take considerably longer once you are off the express roads.
